The MIMIC-IV-ED triage table contains information about the patient when they were first triaged in the emergency department. Patients are assessed at triage by a single care provider and asked a series of questions to assess their current health status. Their vital signs are measured and a level of acuity is assigned. Based on the level of acuity, the patient either waits in the waiting room for later attention, or is prioritized for immediate care.

All fields in the triage table were originally free-text. For deidentification purposes, the vital sign and pain fields were converted into a numeric value. As a result, a small number of NULL values were introduced for text entries. The table stores information collected on triage to the emergency department and has 425,087 rows. It links to the edstays table on stay_id. There is no time associated with triage observations.

The closest approximation to triage time is the intime of the patient from the edstays table. The numeric entries in the triage table were originally stored as free-text, so the columns required deidentification. Free-text entries which could not be converted trivially were removed. As a result, missing data in the numeric columns indicates either deidentified data or no data recorded, although this is usually rare.

In version 2.1, the number of rows missing a temperature value was 23415, of which only 680 had a free-text value deleted during deidentification (about 3 percent). In version 2.1 of the triage table, the other columns had the following numbers of rows missing data. Heart rate had 17090 rows missing, 292 of them NULL values inserted for deidentification. Respiratory rate had 20353 rows missing, 223 inserted for deidentification.

Oxygen saturation had 20596 missing, 414 inserted. Systolic blood pressure had 18291 missing, 238 inserted. Diastolic blood pressure had 19091 missing, 214 inserted. Acuity had 6987 rows missing, none inserted for deidentification. The triage table has the columns subject_id, stay_id, temperature, heartrate, resprate, o2sat, sbp, dbp, pain, acuity and chiefcomplaint. The subject_id is a unique identifier which specifies an individual patient. The stay_id uniquely identifies a single emergency department stay for a single patient.

The temperature column is the patient's temperature in degrees Fahrenheit, and some temperatures may be incorrectly documented as Celsius. The heartrate column is the patient's heart rate in beats per minute. In the triage table, the resprate column is the patient's respiratory rate in breaths per minute. The o2sat column is the patient's peripheral oxygen saturation as a percentage.

The sbp and dbp columns are the patient's systolic and diastolic blood pressure, respectively, measured in millimetres of mercury (mmHg). The pain column is the level of pain self-reported by the patient, on a scale of 0-10. The chiefcomplaint column is a deidentified free-text description of the patient's chief complaint.

The acuity column of the MIMIC-IV-ED triage table is an order of priority based upon acuity utilizing the Emergency Severity Index (ESI) five level triage system. This priority is assigned by a registered nurse. Level 1 is the highest priority, while level 5 is the lowest priority.

For acuity level 1, when a level 1 condition is present or the patient meets ED trigger criteria, the triage process stops, the patient is taken directly to a room and immediate physician intervention is requested. Patient conditions which trigger level 1 include being unresponsive, intubated, apneic, pulseless, requiring a medication or intervention to alter ESI level such as narcan, adenosine or cardioversion, trauma, stroke, and STEMI.

For acuity level 2, when a level 2 condition is identified, the triage nurse notifies the resource nurse and appropriate placement is determined. Patient conditions which trigger level 2 include high risk situations, new onset confusion, suicidal or homicidal ideation, lethargy, seizures or disorientation, possible ectopic pregnancy, an immunocompromised patient with a fever, severe pain or distress, or vital sign instability.

Acuity level 3 includes patients requiring two or more resources (labs, EKG, x-rays, IV fluids, etc) with stable vital signs. Acuity level 4 includes patients requiring one resource only (labs, EKG, etc). Acuity level 5 includes patients not requiring any resources.
